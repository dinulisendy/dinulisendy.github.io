The phone rang while I was on a bus heading back to my hometown. It was the Environmental Director from the Rotaract Club.

"Nangi, can you chair Project Haritha Siripa?" she asked.

It was a major environmental cleanup project at Sripada (Adam's Peak), organized in collaboration with the Rotaract Club of the University of Kelaniya. Without a second thought, my lips said yes. But the moment I hung up, a little voice in my inner mind whispered: *Wait... what are your parents going to say?* You see, I had never even visited Sripada in my entire life. This wasn't just my first time climbing it; I was going to lead a massive cleanup operation there. Thankfully, after a quick conversation, my parents gave me their blessing. That was the green light. The real journey had just begun.

## Building the Team and Funding the Mission

Running a multi-university project means you don't do it alone. I was joined by two co-chairpersons from the Architecture faculty. Sitting together, we brainstormed how to fund this ambitious mission. We decided on a dual approach: printing custom stickers designed by the architecture team and hunting down corporate sponsorships. While I dove into selling those stickers, I also managed to personally secure two solid sponsorships to fund our operations. Everything was falling into place.

Or so I thought.

## The Last-Minute Pivot

Just days before the event, the plot twisted hard. Both of my co-chairs messaged me saying they couldn't participate due to pressing academic deadlines. To make matters worse, the directors couldn't make it either due to unavoidable circumstances. Suddenly, the entire weight of the project rested squarely on my shoulders. Panic tried to creep in, but there was no time for it. With the incredible help of my Organizing Committee, I packed the first-aid kits, organized the heavy-duty gloves, rounded up the garbage bags, and printed the official Rotaract Club of University of Moratuwa posters. Before we set off, our team visited the temple to receive blessings for the journey ahead.

## The Climb

The clock struck 12:00 noon. Our chartered bus rolled into Moratuwa, having already picked up our partners from the University of Kelaniya. After grabbing dinner and refreshments to fuel up, we set off. By the time we reached the starting point and prepared for the climb, around **8:00 PM**, our journey officially began via the Hatton route.

At first, the environment was calm, and the crisp mountain air filled us with excitement. But within minutes, the real adventure — and the real test — began.

The skies opened up, unleashing a torrential downpour. We scrambled to pull on our raincoats, fighting to stay dry as we pushed forward. But the rain was just Act One.

As we climbed higher, we hit a massive human gridlock on the mountain steps. For nearly **two hours**, we were completely stuck in the same spot, unable to move forward as the storm raged on. Time was ticking, and exhaustion was setting in. That's when leadership required a split-second decision. Word reached us of a hidden, unpaved shortcut — a rugged path that could bypass the bottleneck and help us transition toward the Ratnapura route direction. We took the gamble.

There were no streetlights, no paved steps, just slick rocks, thick mud, and rushing water beneath our boots. Navigating that pitch-black shortcut in the pouring rain was terrifying, but it worked. We clawed our way back onto the main trail.

Yet, the mountain wasn't done with us. Just when we thought we were back on track, we slammed into another wall of human traffic near Mahagiri Dambe — getting stuck there for another grueling **two hours**, shoulder-to-shoulder under a relentless storm. Battling howling winds, freezing rain, and massive crowds, every single step upward took sheer willpower.

## At the Summit

We finally clawed our way to the summit, but the intense weather and foot traffic had cost us time: we missed the iconic sunrise (*Irasewaya*). For a brief second, disappointment lingered. But we didn't climb a sacred mountain just to watch the sky; we climbed it to heal it.

The main mission began right then and there. The weather cleared up into a crisp, cooperative calmness. We handed out pairs of gloves and heavy-duty garbage bags to every single member from both UoM and the University of Kelaniya. Spreading out across the temple grounds and surrounding paths, our volunteers bent down and went to work. Plastic wrappers, bottles, and discarded waste that tarnished the holy mountain were systematically cleared away.

Bag after heavy bag, our combined endurance paid off. By the time we handed over the collected waste to the municipal authorities at the base, the trails looked completely transformed.

## The Ride Home

After a well-earned, hearty lunch at the foot of the mountain, we loaded back onto the bus for the ride home to Moratuwa. Looking back at the sleeping, exhausted faces of my UoM peers and our friends from Kelaniya, a profound wave of pride washed over me.

From a sudden phone call on a bus to embarking at 8:00 PM via Hatton, battling rain, taking a terrifying muddy shortcut in the dark, and facing double-gridlocked mountain steps — **Project Haritha Siripa** taught me that when plans fall apart, true grit, teamwork, and resilience will always carry you to the finish line. 🌱✨
